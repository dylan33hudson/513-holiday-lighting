Runs connect with sealed, weatherproof plugs to a single outdoor GFCI outlet and a professional astronomical timer — it turns your display on at dusk and off overnight, on its own, all season. You never touch a switch.

LED or incandescent?

We install commercial-grade C9 bulbs in either style. LED is our default recommendation, but here’s an honest side-by-side so you can choose with your eyes open.

Recommended

LED C9

The lookCrisp, bright warm-white glow
Cost per season~$3–$19
Bulb lifespanRated for multiple seasons
HeatCool to the touch

Efficient and durable — it costs pennies to run all season and is far less likely to need a mid-season fix. Our default for every package.

Classic option

Incandescent C9

The lookDeeper, warmer amber glow
Cost per seasonRoughly 8–10× LED
Bulb lifespanShorter — burns out sooner
HeatRuns noticeably hot

The nostalgic, old-fashioned glow some homeowners grew up with — it just draws far more power and needs more mid-season bulb swaps.

What it costs to run

Running about 6 hours a night for the whole season, at current Cincinnati (Duke Energy) rates, here’s what each package adds to your bill — LED vs. old-style incandescent:

The Classic$3–$6LED · for the entire season$24–$60 incandescent
The Signature$6–$11LED · for the entire season$48–$110 incandescent
The Estate$10–$19LED · for the entire season$80–$190 incandescent

LED estimates based on ~$0.14/kWh all-in Cincinnati rates and typical LED C9 draw; incandescent figures scale that by the standard 8–10× wattage difference between incandescent and LED C9 bulbs. Your actual cost depends on your supply rate and hours run.
